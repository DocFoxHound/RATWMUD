# Run Against the World — World Setting Bible

## Core World Setting Bible — Game Standard v0.1

## 1. High Concept

*Run Against the World* takes place in a pre-industrial civilization built entirely around sapient, quadrupedal wolves.

It is a world of kings, cities, armies, noble houses, churches, merchants, thieves, organized crime, courts, docks, farms, workshops, and ancient institutions. Technology and everyday life resemble an early-modern or late-medieval society, but every part of civilization has developed around canine anatomy rather than human anatomy.

Magic exists, but **this is not a society in which most people are magical**.

The overwhelming majority of wolves possess no supernatural ability. A small minority are born with **Gifts**: usually minute, highly constrained abilities that are more useful as conveniences or occupational advantages than as weapons. Far rarer are **Quickened wolves**, whose manifestation of the same kinds of Gifts exists at an almost unbelievable scale.

The ordinary population generally understands Gifts to be small. A wolf making a cooking flame, manipulating a little clay, or nudging an object toward themselves is unusual but comprehensible.

A wolf capable of incinerating a room, teleporting across enormous distances, flooding a district, or violating another wolf's control over their own body belongs to an entirely different category.

That difference is foundational to the setting.

**RATW should therefore feel like a low-magic world in everyday play, with rare encounters with Quickened wolves suddenly making the supernatural feel enormous and frightening.**

---

## 2. Core World Pillars

### Wolf Civilization

These are not humanoid wolves.

Characters remain quadrupedal wolves, and civilization has evolved around that physiology.

Wolves manipulate the world through combinations of:

- Mouth and teeth
- Forepaws and claws
- Body weight
- Harnesses
- Specialized handles
- Pull-cords
- Low furniture and work surfaces

Doors can use mouth-pull latches. Keys can be manipulated through teeth and foreclaws. Cutlery may be mouth-designed. Homes use floor cushions instead of human chairs, while desks and tables sit low enough for quadrupedal use.

Writing is performed with mouth-held quills. Weapons are designed with mouth grips or body harnesses. Musical instruments can similarly be designed for paws and mouths.

This should affect **every environmental design decision in the game**.

A RATW tavern should not look like a human tavern with wolves placed inside it.

### Scent Matters

Smell is an important component of identity and perception.

Wolves can recognize individuals and locations by scent, notice foreign smells, follow traces, recognize perfumes, detect food or blood from a distance, and potentially expose disguises through smell.

Consequently:

- Bathing has practical implications.
- Perfume can conceal or contaminate scent evidence.
- Clothing carries the scent of its owner and household.
- Infiltration involves smell as well as appearance.
- Blood is difficult to conceal.
- Tracking is naturally more important than it would be in a human society.

This makes scent a potentially useful **gameplay perception layer** alongside sight and sound.

### Body Language Is Language

Ears, tails, posture, hackles, head position, exposed teeth, and physical spacing constantly communicate emotion and social status.

Characters frequently understand one another before anything is spoken.

For animation and dialogue systems, body language should communicate:

- Submission
- Authority
- Fear
- Aggression
- Embarrassment
- Suspicion
- Class etiquette
- Attraction
- Deception
- Readiness to fight

A character lying successfully may therefore require control over their **ears and tail**, not merely convincing dialogue.

## 3. Technological Level

The world should broadly be treated as **late-medieval to early-modern**, without requiring an exact Earth-equivalent century.

Established technologies include:

| Area | World Standard |
|---|---|
| Lighting | Candles, fireplaces, oil lamps |
| Heating | Hearths, fireplaces, wood stoves |
| Writing | Paper, parchment, ink, mouth-held quills, ledgers |
| Weapons | Blades, rapiers, mouth weapons, crossbows, large emplacement crossbows |
| Armor | Leather and similar body-fitted armor |
| Transport | Walking, horse-drawn carriages, boats and ships |
| Industry | Smithing, plasterworks, workshops, mills/trades, docks |
| Communication | Couriers, letters, official records, church/civic bells |
| Construction | Stone, timber, plaster, tile, masonry |
| Government | Administrative offices, records systems, courts, guards, armies |
| Commerce | Markets, merchants, workshops, organized trade |

Do **not** allow magic to casually substitute for technology.

A Fire Wolf does not eliminate fireplaces.

An Earth Wolf does not eliminate masons.

A Blinker does not eliminate couriers.

Most powered wolves are too uncommon and too limited for society to be built around magical labor.

## 4. Social Structure

Wolf society is strongly stratified.

The setting supports:

**Royalty → Nobility / Houses → wealthy merchants and officials → skilled workers → common laborers → urban poor / street population**

Ridgemere demonstrates enormous differences between upper society and its slums. House colors, servants, estates, formal etiquette, guards, and hereditary or institutional privilege coexist with wolves struggling simply to afford food and shelter.

Currency includes silver pennies and larger **marks**; Caltrop understands a mark as roughly ten silver pennies and enough money to live on for a meaningful period.

Social position should influence gameplay almost as much as combat capability.

A completely Normal noble may possess vastly more practical power than a Gifted street wolf.

---

## 5. Government and Political Organization

The world is not presented as one unified kingdom.

Individual states possess their own rulers, institutions, militaries, laws, and cities.

Ser Ferro, for example, possesses:

- A King
- Royal court
- Royal Hand
- Guard
- Captain of the Guard
- Army
- Knightly system
- Administrative bureaucracy
- Records offices
- Clerical institutions
- Noble and merchant houses

Above or between these states exists the **Concord**.

### The Concord

The Concord is a religious-political institution associated with Upper Accord. Ash describes it as a comparatively young organization made up of rulers, kings, and councils, although his description is openly hostile and should not be treated as an impartial account.

Later institutional dialogue establishes that Ser Ferro is formally a **member state of the Concord**, its King sits on a voting council, Concord Representatives can be voted into authority, and appointed agents operate beneath that structure.

The Concord should therefore be treated as something between:

**supranational council + religious authority + diplomatic alliance + regulatory institution.**

It does not necessarily directly administer every member state.

Local kings remain kings.

But the Concord possesses significant authority, particularly concerning religion and Quickened wolves.

## 6. Religion and Cosmology

The dominant religion centers on the **Bright One** and the **Dark One**.

The accepted religious account holds that the Bright One brought structure, purpose, social order, and civilization to wolf-kind; helped establish the first cities; defeated the Dark One's age of chaos and anarchy; and eventually ascended.

The opposition is ideological as much as supernatural:

### Bright One

- Order
- Structure
- Purpose
- Civilization
- Duty
- Community

### Dark One

- Chaos
- Disorder
- Temptation
- Anarchy
- Corruption of proper order

Religious belief is culturally pervasive even among wolves who are not especially observant.

Churches are simultaneously:

- Houses of worship
- Community gathering places
- Charitable institutions
- Archives
- Political institutions
- Centers of education
- Confessional authorities

Religion should be woven into ordinary speech, law, morality, architecture, and cultural assumptions rather than appearing only during explicitly religious quests.

## 7. Important Locations

### Ridgemere

Ridgemere is a sprawling city pressed against mountains and overlooking a bay. Its climate is cold, wet, and oppressive, with near-constant rain broken by snow or unpleasantly humid summers.

Its visual identity should emphasize:

- Gray stone
- Rain
- Fog
- Mud
- Narrow alleys
- Dense construction
- Industrial areas
- Slums
- Bay traffic
- Unequal wealth
- Cramped housing
- Roof overhangs
- Dim interiors

Ridgemere should feel **old, wet, crowded, hungry, and practical**.

### Ser Ferro

Ser Ferro is dramatically different.

It is a large walled metropolis beside a broad river, surrounded on another side by farms and smaller settlements. It possesses extensive docks, white-plastered buildings, red roofs, broad roads, major civic buildings, and a prominent white-and-silver cathedral.

Its identity is:

- White plaster
- Red roofs
- Bright sunlight
- Warm summers
- Stone walls
- Organized streets
- River commerce
- Wharves
- Markets
- Gardens
- Wealth
- Bureaucracy
- Hidden corruption beneath respectable appearances

Ridgemere's underworld is visibly poor and criminal.

Ser Ferro's underworld often wears respectable clothing.

### Upper Accord

Upper Accord is a city associated directly with the Concord and serves as the destination to which the Wardens take powerful wolves for assessment and training.

The Warden Order maintains training grounds in or around Upper Accord, including wilderness training outside the city.

For game purposes, Upper Accord should feel unlike an ordinary national capital.

It is a center of:

- Religious authority
- International politics
- Ancient scholarship
- Warden power
- Quickened containment and training
- Concord administration
- Manuscript archives
- Diplomacy

## 8. Crime and the Underworld

Organized crime is sophisticated.

Ser Ferro's **Syndicate** deliberately acts through proxy organizations such as the Couriers so that those lower organizations absorb enforcement pressure while the Syndicate remains insulated.

Crime therefore operates through networks of:

- Couriers
- Burglars
- Fixers
- Smugglers
- Legitimate businesses
- Corrupt officials
- Informants
- Fences
- Proxy gangs
- Blackmail
- Bribery
- Political connections

This fits the larger theme of RATW:

**The most consequential forms of power are often indirect.**

A wolf does not have to breathe fire to control a city.

## 9. The Magic System

### Fundamental Rule: Classification and Gift Family Are Different Things

Every character has a **Power Classification**.

Every supernatural character also has exactly **one Gift Family**.

These are two separate variables.

For example:

**Normal**
- No Gift Family

**Gifted Fire Wolf**
- Power Classification: Gifted
- Gift Family: Fire

**Quickened Fire Wolf**
- Power Classification: Quickened
- Gift Family: Fire

A Quickened Fire Wolf does **not** become capable of blinking, controlling earth, raising corpses, or manipulating water.

They remain a Fire Wolf.

They are simply a terrifyingly powerful expression of that same Gift.

This should be a hard worldbuilding rule.

---

## 10. The Three Power Classifications

### I. Normal

**Definition:** A wolf possessing no supernatural Gift.

This should represent the overwhelming majority of the population.

Normal wolves include:

- Soldiers
- Officers
- Kings
- Nobles
- Craftsmen
- Criminals
- Priests
- Merchants
- Farmers
- Scholars
- Guards
- Thieves

Normal does **not** mean weak.

A veteran Normal soldier should be significantly more dangerous in an ordinary fight than an untrained Gifted wolf whose supernatural talent is only useful for lighting candles.

Skills, wealth, training, equipment, authority, knowledge, and relationships all exist independently of magic.

#### Gameplay Principle

**Normal characters progress through skill.**

Their strength comes from:

- Physical conditioning
- Stealth
- Weapons
- Armor
- Leadership
- Knowledge
- Craftsmanship
- Wealth
- Reputation
- Social authority
- Specialized training

### II. Gifted

**Definition:** A wolf born with a genuine supernatural ability whose normal manifestation remains relatively small.

The novel establishes Gifts as uncommon but not unbelievable. In Ser Ferro, only a few hundred wolves among roughly one hundred thousand possess Gifts at all, and their abilities are catalogued into recognized groups.

Gifted abilities commonly appear useful rather than catastrophic.

Examples established very early include:

- Making a small cooking flame
- Manipulating clay
- Nudging an object slightly
- Short-distance blinking

A Gifted wolf might therefore build an occupation around their Gift without becoming a military asset.

#### Example

Fixer is explicitly called a **Fire Wolf**, but his ability amounts to producing a small flame near his paw while holding his breath. It is convenient for lighting his pipe—not destroying buildings.

#### Gameplay Principle

**Gifted characters possess utility magic, not unrestricted spellcasting.**

Their Gift should provide:

- shortcuts,
- specialized interaction,
- occupational advantages,
- clever combat opportunities,
- traversal options,
- or situational advantages.

It should not replace their mundane skillset.

### III. Quickened

**Definition:** An extraordinarily rare wolf whose Gift manifests at an extreme level far beyond its ordinary Gifted expression.

Quickened wolves are rarer than Gifted wolves by a substantial degree. Weaver, despite significant government experience, remembers seeing only one before Caltrop, while Wardens themselves remain in extremely short supply because Quickened wolves are so rare.

Quickened wolves are not simply “better Gifted wolves.”

Their Gift may reach a point at which its implications become almost metaphysical.

Caltrop's blinking progresses from simple transportation into transporting another wolf and interacting with consciousness, identity, memory, and the separation between mind and body.

Quickened wolves should therefore feel like **a Gift reaching its frightening logical extreme**.

#### Gameplay Principle

Quickened characters are not routine elite enemies.

They are events.

Their appearance should change how other characters behave.

Governments care.

The Concord cares.

Wardens care.

Criminal organizations care.

Ordinary wolves may simply run.

---

## 11. Quickened Is Not a Character Level

**Game-world standardization**

A Gifted wolf should not become Quickened simply because they gained enough experience points.

Quickening is an intrinsic supernatural classification or potential.

Training can improve:

- control
- accuracy
- efficiency
- recovery
- endurance
- creativity
- understanding

But training should not normally transform a minor Gift into Quickening.

This preserves the extreme rarity established by the setting.

A Quickened player character may **unlock greater control over something they always possessed**, but they are not acquiring Quickening through an RPG skill tree.

---

## 12. Universal Laws of Gifts

The book explicitly establishes that Gifts possess three attributes:

### Limit

What the Gift cannot normally exceed.

For an ordinary blinker, this includes extremely short range.

### Cost

What using the Gift does to its user.

Blinking causes effects including:

- Dizziness
- Nausea
- Disorientation
- Confusion

### Tell

The condition or behavior necessary to invoke the Gift.

An ordinary blinker requires weightlessness, usually achieved through a hop, jump, or fall.

Caltrop is abnormal because her range is vastly greater and she does not require the usual tell.

### Game Standard

**Every Gift in RATW must define all three.**

There is no ability whose design begins and ends with:

> “This wolf controls fire.”

Instead:

> **Family:** Fire  
> **Limit:** Small flame near the body  
> **Cost:** Cannot breathe during generation  
> **Tell:** Breath held + raised paw  
> **Classification:** Gifted

This makes Gifts feel biological, strange, and grounded rather than like conventional spellcasting.

---

## 13. One Wolf — One Magic Family

This should be one of the hardest rules in the game setting.

A supernatural wolf receives **one Gift Family**.

They do not multiclass between magical domains.

A Fire Wolf cannot later unlock Earth magic.

A Blinker cannot learn Water manipulation.

A Death Walker cannot learn Wind abilities.

Quickening expands the **depth of one domain**, never the number of domains.

This also means extraordinary abilities should first be interpreted through the wolf's existing Gift rather than by assigning them another power.

Caltrop's increasingly supernatural interactions with distance, bodies, minds, and souls remain expressions of what it fundamentally means for a **Blinker to move something from one place/state to another**.

That is a much more distinctive magic system than conventional collections of spells.

---

## 14. Standard Gift Family Registry

The game should maintain a **closed registry of Gift Families**.

New individual NPCs should not receive randomly invented powers.

If a new supernatural effect is needed, it must belong to an existing family or a new family must be formally added to the world bible.

### Fire Wolves — Canon

**Domain:** Heat, ignition, fire.

#### Gifted

Possible scale:

- Light a candle.
- Ignite tinder.
- Warm an object.
- Sustain a palm-sized flame.
- Apply concentrated heat to a small target.

Fixer represents this scale.

#### Quickened

Possible scale:

- Project torrents of fire.
- Ignite rooms or structures.
- Produce concussive thermal effects.
- Sustain combat-effective flame.
- Rapidly consume large areas.

Veska demonstrates the enormous difference between an ordinary Fire Wolf and a Quickened one.

#### Prohibition

Fire Wolves do not automatically receive:

- Lightning
- Light magic
- Earth manipulation
- Explosive telekinesis

Anything they accomplish must still be explainable through **fire and heat**.

### Blinkers — Canon

**Domain:** Spatial displacement.

Blinking is one of the rarest recognized Gifts. Ser Ferro's records contain no known local blinker when Caltrop arrives.

#### Gifted

Typical:

- Several body lengths.
- Possibly across a doorway or obstacle.
- Requires weightlessness.
- Causes significant dizziness/disorientation.
- Recovery prevents constant teleportation.

#### Quickened

Caltrop demonstrates the potential for:

- Extreme range.
- Chained blinks.
- Activation without normal weightlessness.
- Rapid recovery compared with ordinary blinkers.
- Transporting another wolf.
- Extreme dissociative states.
- Interaction with another wolf's consciousness during displacement.
- Eventually manipulating another mind through the deeper implications of her Gift.

A Quickened Blinker therefore does not become “a psychic.”

Instead, the **spatial concept of separation, location, body, consciousness, and placement becomes increasingly profound**.

### Water Wolves — Author-Specified / Canon-Supported

**Domain:** Water and liquid motion.

The manuscript does not explicitly use the title “Water Wolf,” but Weaver remembers a Quickened pup whose uncontrolled power produced devastating water damage, strongly supporting a water-related Gift family.

#### Gifted

Appropriate examples:

- Pull a small quantity of water.
- Redirect a trickle.
- Move water from one vessel to another.
- Manipulate a shallow current.
- Extract or gather nearby free water in small quantities.

#### Quickened

Appropriate examples:

- Generate or redirect powerful flows.
- Flood streets or buildings.
- Manipulate river-scale currents locally.
- Strike with high-pressure water.
- Create waves or moving masses of water.

#### Important Limitation

Water Wolves should not automatically gain:

- Ice
- Weather
- Healing
- Blood manipulation

Those are separate domains unless deliberately incorporated into the final family definition.

### Wind Wolves — Author-Specified

**Domain:** Air movement and pressure.

#### Gifted

Appropriate examples:

- Produce a small breeze.
- Push smoke aside.
- Dry damp fur.
- Move light objects.
- Assist a sail.
- Nudge something barely out of reach.

#### Quickened

Appropriate examples:

- Violent gusts.
- Throw wolves off their feet.
- Redirect projectiles.
- Create dangerous pressure changes.
- Control large volumes of moving air.
- Produce localized storm-force winds.

#### Prohibition

Wind does not automatically mean:

- Lightning
- Weather control
- Flight
- Sound magic

Those effects require explicit connection to the domain and world rules.

### Earth Wolves — Author-Specified / Thematically Supported

**Domain:** Soil, stone, clay, and terrestrial material.

The manuscript's mundane Gift examples include magically shaping clay, which provides an excellent Gifted-scale expression for this family.

#### Gifted

Appropriate examples:

- Shape wet clay.
- Loosen packed soil.
- Smooth stone imperfections.
- Move pebbles.
- Assist masonry or pottery.
- Detect unusual stress in nearby stone.

#### Quickened

Appropriate examples:

- Raise stone barriers.
- Tear apart masonry.
- Collapse structures.
- Open fissures.
- Reshape terrain.
- Throw large masses of rock or earth.

A Gifted Earth Wolf might simply be an exceptionally valuable potter.

A Quickened Earth Wolf might threaten a fortress.

That contrast is exactly what the system should preserve.

### Healers / Blood Wolves — Canon, Taxonomy to Standardize

Veska explicitly introduces Hemlock as a **healer**.

His actual ability reveals a much deeper connection to blood: after biting Caltrop, he creates strands of her blood between them and uses those connections to control her body like a marionette.

For the game taxonomy, I recommend treating **Healing and Blood Manipulation as the same underlying Gift Family** rather than two unrelated powers.

Common terminology can still call benign practitioners **Healers**.

#### Gifted

Appropriate examples:

- Slow bleeding.
- Encourage clotting.
- Accelerate minor healing.
- Manipulate circulation locally.
- Stabilize an injured wolf.

#### Quickened

Appropriate examples:

- Rapid or extraordinary healing.
- Manipulate another wolf through their blood.
- Control circulation and bodily movement.
- Perform otherwise impossible biological intervention.

The important principle is that healing is not generic holy magic.

It is a manifestation of **physical life and blood**.

### Death Walkers — Author-Specified

**Domain:** Death, corpses, and the remnants of the dead.

Death Walkers do not appear by that name in the current Book 1 manuscript, so their exact boundaries should be regarded as **game/world canon being added here at your direction**, rather than something derived from this manuscript.

They should function as RATW's closest equivalent to necromancers.

#### Gifted

Keep them appropriately minute:

- Sense whether something nearby recently died.
- Feel the presence of a corpse without seeing it.
- Momentarily twitch or move a recently dead small animal.
- Perceive an emotional or spiritual residue associated with death.
- Preserve a corpse slightly longer than normal.
- Receive a fragmentary impression from something recently dead.

A Gifted Death Walker should be eerie and useful.

Not an army-raising necromancer.

#### Quickened

A Quickened Death Walker may:

- Animate corpses.
- Direct multiple dead bodies.
- Interact with persistent fragments of the deceased.
- Temporarily bind a dead wolf to a body.
- Question or manipulate remnants of the dead.
- Exert power across an area containing many corpses.

Exactly how much genuine consciousness survives death should remain an important metaphysical question rather than becoming routine videogame knowledge.

#### Prohibition

Death Walking is not automatically:

- Healing
- Blood control
- Mind control of the living
- Teleportation
- Shadow elemental magic

Keep those families separate.

## 16. Quickened Design Philosophy

The most important distinction is not merely magnitude.

Quickening should reveal the **deeper meaning of a Gift**.

For example:

**Fire**
- Gifted: create fire.
- Quickened: become a devastating source of fire and heat.

**Earth**
- Gifted: manipulate a little clay.
- Quickened: treat buildings and terrain as mutable material.

**Blink**
- Gifted: relocate yourself several feet.
- Quickened: challenge the very distinction between location, body, and consciousness.

**Blood**
- Gifted: help a wound close.
- Quickened: treat a living body as a system whose movement can be rewritten.

**Death**
- Gifted: sense death.
- Quickened: challenge whether death truly ends a wolf's agency.

This gives each Quickened family a unique kind of horror and wonder.

---

## 17. Costs Should Scale With Power

Quickened should never mean unlimited.

Caltrop's extreme blinking causes severe nausea, bleeding, disassociation, confusion, and the frightening possibility of losing connection with her own body.

Therefore:

**The more extraordinary the feat, the more dangerous the Cost should become.**

Possible costs across families include:

- Exhaustion
- Dehydration
- Inability to breathe
- Heat injury
- Physical pain
- Blood loss
- Temporary paralysis
- Disorientation
- Loss of consciousness
- Dissociation
- Sensory overload
- Loss of fine control
- Risk of harming unintended targets

This makes Quickened characters dangerous without making them effortless superheroes.

---

## 18. Tells Are Important for Combat

A Tell should matter mechanically.

Enemies who understand the Gift can recognize and interrupt it.

Examples:

- Blinker must hop.
- Fire Wolf must hold breath.
- Blood Wolf must make physical/blood contact.
- Earth Wolf might require direct ground contact.
- Wind Wolf might require an inhalation/exhalation cycle.
- Death Walker might require proximity or attention to a corpse.

Quickened wolves may possess altered or weakened Tells, which is itself frightening.

Caltrop's lack of the normal blinking Tell is one reason she is so difficult to contain.

---

## 19. The Warden Order

Wardens are **not a Gift Family**.

They are an institution.

The Order predates the Concord but now serves beneath it. Wardens wear distinctive black cloaks with silver trim and are sufficiently famous or feared that experienced wolves recognize them.

Their stated purpose includes:

- Finding powerful wolves
- Assessing them
- Training them
- Protecting them
- Protecting others from them
- Giving them purpose

But this protection is coercive.

By Veska's understanding, Quickened wolves cannot simply be permitted to live independently because their power presents too great a danger.

Thus:

**Quickened discovery → Warden interest → assessment → recruitment, containment, or confrontation.**

This creates an excellent systemic world rule.

Using extraordinary magic publicly can have consequences beyond immediate witnesses.

---

## 20. Gifts and Law

Sophisticated governments know Gifts exist.

Ser Ferro catalogs Gifted wolves and classifies them into known groupings.

Therefore authorities may maintain:

- Gift censuses
- Identification records
- Specialist containment cells
- Military doctrine for Gifted enemies
- Warden contact procedures
- Known-Gift watchlists
- Investigative records

However, knowledge is uneven.

An ordinary wolf may know that Fire Wolves exist while having no meaningful understanding of what a Quickened Fire Wolf can do.

This difference between **bureaucratic knowledge and common knowledge** is important.

---

## 21. Magic and Society

Gifted wolves should appear throughout ordinary society.

A small Gift can affect occupation.

Examples:

**Fire**  
→ cooks, smiths, lamplighters, criminals

**Earth**  
→ potters, builders, masons

**Water**  
→ sailors, farmers, brewers, firefighters

**Wind**  
→ sailors, couriers, mill workers

**Healer/Blood**  
→ medicine

**Blink**  
→ couriers, thieves, scouts

**Death Walker**  
→ mortuary work, battlefield recovery, religious controversy

However, a Gift should never determine a character's personality or profession automatically.

A Fire Wolf may be an accountant.

A Blinker may hate traveling.

A Death Walker may be terrified of corpses.

---

## 22. Combat Philosophy

RATW combat should remain physical.

Most fights involve:

- Teeth
- Body weight
- Claws
- Blades
- Crossbows
- Armor
- Terrain
- Surprise
- Numbers
- Position
- Training

Magic supplements that system.

It should not turn every confrontation into ranged spell combat.

This also allows Normal wolves to remain dangerous.

A trained Normal officer with a blade can be far more dangerous than a Gifted Wind Wolf whose entire ability is producing a stiff breeze.

## 26. Gameplay Rarity Standard

Do not populate a game map with Quickened wolves merely because the game requires enemy variety.

A useful hierarchy is:

### Normal

Routine population.

### Gifted

Uncommon enough that a Gift remains interesting, but common enough that cities know how to categorize them.

Ser Ferro provides a useful benchmark: **only a few hundred among roughly 100,000 wolves.**

### Quickened

Hand-authored.

Named.

Narratively significant.

A Quickened NPC should almost never be procedural background population.

Their existence should affect factions, rumors, government records, and Warden activity.

## 27. World Design Rules

For consistency, new content should obey the following:

1. **Most wolves have no powers.**
2. **Gifted wolves have one Gift Family.**
3. **Quickened wolves also have one Gift Family.**
4. **Quickening increases the depth and magnitude of that family; it does not give additional schools of magic.**
5. **Every Gift requires a Limit, Cost, and Tell.**
6. **Training improves control; it does not ordinarily create new supernatural domains.**
7. **Gifted abilities should usually be small enough to fit into everyday society.**
8. **Quickened abilities should feel startling even to wolves familiar with Gifts.**
9. **Normal characters remain extremely relevant because political, martial, economic, and social power are separate from supernatural power.**
10. **Magic must not erase the need for ordinary technology, professions, armies, or institutions.**
11. **The Warden Order is an institution, not a magic class.**
12. **Extraordinary public use of Quickened power should attract institutional consequences.**
13. **Every magical effect introduced into the game must map to an approved Gift Family.**
14. **Do not invent one-off powers simply to make an NPC unique. Make the wolf unique through personality, skill, circumstances, and the particular expression of an established Gift.**
15. **The world should remain recognizably RATW even in a scene containing no magic whatsoever.**

## 28. One-Sentence World Standard

**Run Against the World is an early-modern civilization of quadrupedal wolves in which kings, churches, cities, crime, class, and ordinary skill shape everyday life; a small minority possess tightly constrained supernatural Gifts belonging to fixed magical families, while extraordinarily rare Quickened wolves manifest those same Gifts at frightening scales and are consequently sought, trained, controlled, or hunted by the ancient Warden Order and the Concord.**
