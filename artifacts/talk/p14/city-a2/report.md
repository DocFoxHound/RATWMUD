## Voices (one line each: what makes this wolf unlike any other in the town)
- rm148 the flood-chalk baker: bakes before light near the north gate and talks the whole time it takes: loud, blunt, honest to the crumb, first with the carts' news, and sure which streets go under first in a downpour, chalked fresh on the oven door every wet morning. Oath "Barm and brimstone"; saying "Water finds the low step first".
- rm149 the peg-whittler: is a watchful, close-mouthed wolf of Northgate who tries every latch twice and whittles a peg for any that rattles, keeps an old map with the roads drawn wrong, and trusts a warm loaf more than a new face. Oath "Nicks and notches"; saying "Try it twice".
- rm150 the cut-reckoner: keeps a counter in the Commons, frets over every small thing twice, and reckons every debt, owed or owing, in cuts of meat: a shank here, half a shoulder there, and none of it ever forgotten. Oath "Shank and shoulder"; saying "Paid is the only clean word".
- rm151 the square-by-dark: helps behind a counter in the Commons and will not sleep on a debt, owed or owing: quiet, short with anyone who says "tomorrow", stubborn as a stuck door, and given to standing still in an autumn wind as if somebody had called from a long way off. Oath "Crusts and quittance"; saying "A debt goes stale quicker than a loaf".
- rm152 the pup with the dry rag: keeps a coat brushed spotless, a small knife sharp and a dry rag in one pocket for wiping benches, sills and blades, says only what is needed, and watches the roof birds set every feather straight. Oath "Spots and splashes"; saying "Clean goes further".
- rm153 the pin-chart keeper: draws charts of the Sound for a Commons counter: exact, unsmiling and ambitious, short with haggling and worse with waiting, and every ship that comes in gets a pin in the harbour chart on the wall. Oath "Lead and tallow"; saying "The water doesn't care what you drew".
- rm154 the edge kept for nothing: sits high over the north gate road and misses nothing: blunt, curious and talkative, poor as a gutter and not ashamed of it, fond of the slate birds and a white misty morning, and forever drawing an old knife over a little grey stone though there is nothing left to cut. Oath "Spit and stone"; saying "Waiting's a job for the dead".
- rm155 the usher with the sooty paw: works the long waiting bench as an usher, speaks low and in order, carries a lump of coal for luck and wipes the black off the paw before every bow, is tired most mornings, and comes alive only at a festival dance. Oath "Patience, patience"; saying "Every name gets called in the end".
- rm156 the rail-counter: keeps a butcher's block by the north gate and counts the fourteen hooks on the rail twice before unbarring: small, quick and anxious, plain at the scales, apt to drift off mid-cut, fond of honey cakes, an empty chapel and a bout on the green. Oath "Salt and sorrow"; saying "Twice round the rail and then to bed".
- rm157 the wave in the weave: weaves by the north gate and lives over the loom: calm, sparing with words, fond of a cold swim and a jar of pickled herring, and every bolt off that loom has a thin blue wave worked into its edge. Oath "Warp and water"; saying "A wave goes in every bolt".
- rm158 the even-slicer: cuts every share into pieces exactly the same size, bread, cheese or apple, and takes offence if anyone calls it fussing: a careful, touchy, unsmiling Northgate pup who listens to old tales, dreams of a sharp knife, and trusts no House further than the end of its own cart. Oath "Even slices"; saying "A crooked cut starts a quarrel".
- rm159 the paw-span measurer: works long days with timber, says little and most of it dry, swims in cold water for the pleasure of it, and sizes up every table, door and wolf in paw spans before passing judgement. Oath "Span and a half"; saying "Measure first, grumble after".
- rm160 the coal in the hem: cuts and stitches coats behind a Commons counter: chatty, teasing and a shade too free with a compliment, whittles buttons by the fire of an evening, has an eye on the Heights, and sews a crumb of coal into every hem without telling a soul. Oath "Snip and tuck"; saying "Everybody's a size, if you're kind about it".
- rm161 the shutter against the damp: minds a cloth counter near the north gate: touchy and anxious, quick to hear a slight that was never meant, folds every bolt square, takes riddles very seriously, loves a cup of spiced cider on a long summer evening, and slams the window shut on the damp while insisting it was only the flies. Oath "Flies take it"; saying "A shut window never caught a cold".
- rm162 the honey-not-sugar preserver: puts up honey preserves on the Commons, swears by honey over sugar, calls everybody "my bee", talks till the tea goes cold, and answers every cough in the damp with a spoonful from the crock, which is only tasting, mind. Oath "Sweet Bright One"; saying "Patient honey, greedy sugar".
- rm163 the dose said back: keeps an apothecary's counter on the Commons below the Rise and will not hand over so much as a twist of willow bark until the dose has been said back: unhurried, cool, guarded, sparing with words, and soft only about smoked eel, the birds on the ridge tiles and a long summer evening. Oath "Gall and gentian"; saying "Say it back or leave it".
- rm164 the kindling-giver: helps in a workshop by the north gate and bags up the shavings every evening as kindling for whoever on the street is short: gentle, blunt, warm, and never one to dress a thing up. Oath "Curls and kindling"; saying "Nothing's waste if a neighbour can burn it".
- rm165 the candle-namer: lifts and dips for a chandler by the north gate and says one neighbour's name to the Bright One over every candle: blunt, devout, humourless, quick to pass on what the travellers bring in and slow to dress it up, fond of the roof birds and no friend at all to the gulls or the wind off the sea. Oath "Bright One witness"; saying "A crooked prayer burns crooked".
- rm166 the riddle at the rag sacks: buys and sells second-hand rags, bones and buttons, talks nineteen to the dozen, worries a thing twice over, and will knock a penny off for any wolf who can answer a riddle, but never for one who haggles. Oath "Holes and patches"; saying "Somebody wore it once and somebody will again".
- rm167 the weather in the chest: dries apples and seals crocks in a loft under the slates: talkative, curious and dry as a good ring of apple, tidy to a fault, and gives the coming weather by the catch in that chest, which is never, ever a cough. Oath "Sulphur and string"; saying "Sugar forgives, damp never does".
- rm168 the backward walker: runs a ropewalk near the north gate for the House that owns it: merry and exact, quick with a tease and slow to lose count, wears out boot heels walking backward down the shed out of long habit, plays a wicked game of draughts, and keeps an eye on a door up the Rise. Oath "Twist and tally"; saying "Count the fathoms, never the fuss".
- rm169 the coal under the first stone: keeps the stone yard's tally for its House, talks little and softly, sets a lump of coal under the first stone of every job for luck, loves a misty dawn and a song round a table, and is putting coin by, one at a time, for a cider stall on the Commons. Oath "Coal and keystone"; saying "Everything stands on something".
- rm170 the bareheaded scholar: is a quiet, dry-witted wolf of Northgate who takes the hood off when the rain comes warm, copies at the abbey school, fishes off the pier of an evening, and says the true thing plainly and then nothing more. Oath "Ink and puddles"; saying "Warm rain never hurt a scholar".
- rm171 the landing dreamer: lives near the north gate on very little, says not much and smiles at most of it, and stops on every landing of the long stairs to choose, once again, which grand house up on the Rise is going to be the last home. Oath "Crackling and candlelight"; saying "Looking up costs nothing".
- rm172 the petition-copier: lost a petition somewhere in the City Office, copies it out again from memory every Restday and carries it back to the same window each week: soft-spoken, slow to answer, often a long way off in thought, content with little, and worn thin only by queues and by noise in the night. Oath "Ribbon and wax"; saying "It'll turn up when it's tired of hiding".
- rm173 the boat-loaf baker: is up before the bells at the ovens, says little and gives away less, and shapes the last scrap of each dawn's dough into a small boat for whoever is out on the water. Oath "Crust and keel"; saying "The dough keeps its own clock".
- rm174 the fever-reader: mixes syrups and willow tea at a Commons counter: quiet, dreamy and quick to take offence, self-taught from a borrowed book, hums over the mortar, and can name every street that flooded by the fevers that climb out of it a week after. Oath "Bitter aloes"; saying "The body says it, if you'll hush and listen".
- rm175 the promise-shaker: is a warm, quick-to-sting wolf of the Commons who swims in any water however cold, loves honey cakes and the festival bells, takes a slight to heart and forgives it just as fast, and will not let a promise pass without paws shaken on it. Oath "Paws and promises"; saying "A shook promise stays shook".
- rm176 the rain-cup pup: sets a cup on the windowsill every time it rains and marks how high the water comes, keeps secrets close, shares oatcakes freely, makes up riddles nobody can answer, and wonders whether anywhere in the realm the rain ever stops. Oath "Puddles and pips"; saying "It has to stop sometime".
- rm177 the straw on the step: helps behind a provisioner's counter near the north gate: warm, careful and plain, forks fresh straw onto the step each dawn against the cart mud, keeps every sack turned label-out, and asks after travellers like old friends. Oath "Fork and broom"; saying "What comes in on the paw goes out on the broom".
- rm178 the bent-pane looker: helps at a glassworks in the Commons, careful and unhurried, singing with friends when the day's done, and holds every flawed pane up to the light to see how it bends the street behind it. Oath "Cullet and cinders"; saying "Every pane tells the street a little wrong".
- rm179 the overnight cooper: keeps a cooperage in the Commons and lives over it, and never sells a cask until it has stood a night full of water: gentle, warm, honest to a fault, quick to tell a buyer the one thing wrong with a cask and slow to say anything unkind, fond of warm bread, a reel on a festival night and the chapel when nobody else is in it. Oath "Hoop and heartwood"; saying "If it weeps, you'll hear it from me".
- rm180 the coin-biter: talks loud and fast, trusts nobody until proven, bites every coin that comes near to see if it is true, hunts the wrong roads on old maps, and is forever counting the stars. Oath "Gold or tin"; saying "If it bends, it's bad".
- rm181 the pine on the crust: bakes for a counter in the Market Hall by the north gate: blunt, ambitious and unsmiling, first to hear what the North Road carts bring, short with crowds and wet floors, and cuts a little pine tree into the crust of every loaf. Oath "Rye and resin"; saying "Bread's sold by six or not at all".
- rm182 the asker of Houses: is a narrow-eyed, close-mouthed wolf of the Commons who wants to know which House a stranger serves before giving anything back, loves a thunderstorm heard from under a sound roof and an empty chapel, swims where nobody else does, and cannot abide gulls or a crowd. Oath "Thunder take it"; saying "Say your House first".
- rm183 the doorpost reckoner: sits by the door with a small knife and cuts a notch in the post for every debt owed or owing, crosses it through when paid, trusts few faces, likes a honey cake and a riddle, and says less than is known. Oath "Notch and nail"; saying "A notch never forgets".
- rm184 the voice across the square: is a big-hearted, big-voiced wolf of the Commons who would sooner shout across Market Square than walk it, runs on about crackling, new boots and the stars over the Rise, and stands up loudly for kin and friends alike. Oath "Crackling and candlewax"; saying "Say it loud or don't bother".
