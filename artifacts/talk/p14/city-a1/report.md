## Voices (one line each: what makes this wolf unlike any other in the town)
- rm111 the one-peel gate-sitter: is one of the city's old ones: devout, blunt and sparing with words, watching what comes in by the north road, singing at chapel, and paring every apple in one long peel for a prayer. Oath "Pips and prayers"; saying "Waiting's the one sin we're all made to practise".
- rm112 the one-line chorus: keeps a busy house by the north gate: quick, teasing and frank, first with whatever news comes off the carts, proud of the stew pot and the carving knife, quick to joke and slow to talk about self, and forever humming one line of a sea song for the room to finish. Oath "Strike me sober"; saying "News comes in wet and goes out dry".
- rm113 the first-star provisioner: sells goods that keep from boards by the water and climbs home at dusk: patient, proud and even-voiced, judging most goods by how long each keeps, and never shutting up shop before the first star is out. Oath "Salt hold it"; saying "Time does the curing".
- rm114 the ear at the north gate: sits under the north gate arch most days and knows each north road cart by its wheels: few words, a shut door to faces not known, a tin of coin put by for a market stall, and barley soup when the rain comes warm. Oath "Hold the axle"; saying "The wheel speaks before the carter does".
- rm115 the cold pipe: sits at the door on the Commons with a clay pipe that hasn't been lit in years and still smells of it: dry, warm, loyal to the street, fond of a crisp apple, an evening line off the sea wall and the festival bells. Oath "Clay and cinders"; saying "A cold pipe keeps its smoke longest".
- rm116 the rig-namer at the block: talks fast and takes offence faster, is always first with whatever came through Northgate, keeps a knife that would split a hair, and calls every joint on the block after a kind of ship. Oath "Sawdust and sails"; saying "You can't sell yesterday's news nor yesterday's mutton".
- rm117 the cart-clock: is one of the city's old ones, sits where the carts come in off the north road and tells the hour by the wheels, blunt, exact and sparing with words, and never once wrong about a count. Oath "By the twelfth cart"; saying "A gate keeps better time than a bell".
- rm118 the eyes-shut whittler: sits at the edge of the market with a knife and a bit of soft wood, eyes shut, listening to the city haggle: slow, gentle, few in words, content with bread and a roof, and short of patience only with folk who keep others waiting. Oath "Shavings and sawdust"; saying "Let it come at its own pace".
- rm119 the peg of spare hoods: teaches letters and sums by the north gate and writes letters for anyone who cannot: kind, plain-spoken and steady, quick to feed a caller and slow to judge, with a peg of spare oilcloth hoods by the door for every pupil who comes without. Oath "Mercy, my slates"; saying "Letters keep, words wander".
- rm120 the banner-reader: helps behind a family counter on the Commons and chafes at it: short-tempered, restless and touchy about manners, reading the House banners over the Rain Hall every morning for the quarrel sure to come. Oath "Needle and nap"; saying "Four Houses at one table, and too few chairs".
- rm121 the hair-tested edge: keeps a counter on the Commons and tries every blade on a single hair before the first cut of the day: quiet, shrewd at the scale, slow to say what anything weighed, fond of blackberries and a riddle worth the trouble. Oath "Fat and gristle"; saying "The scale says what the scale says".
- rm122 the key-ring keeper: keeps the long store by the north gate with a ring of keys known one from another by weight alone: exact, devout, reserved and humourless, happiest with a full larder, a spade in the evening and the tally square. Oath "Bright One square the count"; saying "A key you can't name is a door you've lost".
- rm123 the top-landing pride: is proud, upright and anxious, keeps a decent coat and a civil tongue, sang bass in the old part-songs, and will never be caught resting on a stair while anyone can see. Oath "Bright One mend my knees"; saying "Manners cost nothing, and they're all I've left".
- rm124 the damp-feeler of the yard: runs a builders' yard for the House that owns it and answers for every load: warm, kindly and forever fretting, glancing up at the clouds, pressing a paw to the brick stacks for damp, and pressing apples on anyone who stops. Oath "Tarps and tiles"; saying "Cover it first and fret after".
- rm125 the loaf-knocker: bakes for Market Square and runs the counter like a ship: quick to flare at bad manners and quicker to feed anyone who looks hungry, proud of a decent loaf, fond of old maps and a dance, and never lets a loaf go out without knocking on it first. Oath "Flour and fury"; saying "Knock it and it'll tell you the truth".
- rm126 the honey-cake reckoner: watches the market from the steps, plays dice behind the stalls, says little and misses nothing, and keeps every debt in the city reckoned in honey cakes: owed or owing, it is all written in a head that never forgets a crumb. Oath "Snake's eyes"; saying "A cake owed is a cake owed".
- rm127 the frost-ship drawer: is a quiet, faraway pup of the Commons who draws ships with one claw on frost, slate and paper, dreams of a berth on a ship bound somewhere dry, and has never once put a paw in deep water. Oath "Fair wind and far"; saying "Somewhere it isn't raining".
- rm128 the charcoal copyist: copies every notice posted in the square with a stub of charcoal, letter for letter, to learn a clerk's hand: prickly, stubborn, set on a stool at the City Office, fond of smoked eel, a song with others, and a misty dawn. Oath "Rub it out"; saying "Nobody hands you a stool for waiting".
- rm129 the doorpost bell-tapper: taps every chapel bell out on the nearest doorpost, keeps a riddle ready for anyone who stops, and says what is true even when it would be kinder not to: calm, gentle and plain, fond of blackberries, the empty chapel and the first snow. Oath "Tap and toll"; saying "Riddles are only hard from the outside".
- rm130 the clerk who times the rain: writes a square, careful hand, answers exactly what was asked and not a word more, times every shower against the court clock, and is the last wolf anybody would expect to see dancing a reel on a feast night. Oath "Entered and witnessed"; saying "Paper remembers what folk forget".
- rm131 the roof-bird whittler: is a watchful, close-mouthed wolf of the Commons who trusts few faces, dreams up on the rooftops with the birds, and is never without a little knife and a stick of kindling turning slowly into a sparrow. Oath "Sparrow's elbows"; saying "Birds don't tell".
- rm132 the pothole namer: drives loads up and down the city's steep streets and talks the whole way: warm, teasing, poor and cheerful about it, fond of a cold swim and a loud storm, with a pet name for every hole in the road. Oath "Wheels and whistles"; saying "Know your holes and the road's your friend".
- rm133 the carver of pairs: whittles little animals out of offcuts, always two of a kind so none stands alone, puts every spare coin by for a stall to sell the pairs from, sits in the chapel in the empty hours, speaks plainly and patiently, takes offence quietly and holds it long, and has picked a House to stand by and trusts nobody in another's colours. Oath "Knots and burrs"; saying "Cut with the grain".
- rm134 the copper-tin pup at the oven: is a stocky, dry little pup of Northgate who thumps the bottom of every loaf to hear whether it rings hollow, counts coppers into a tin under the bed for a stall of #self.first#'s own, and bristles at anyone who says small. Oath "Burnt bottoms"; saying "Knock twice, then listen".
- rm135 the tile-counter in the smoke: runs a tile kiln and frets over every firing, every load and every crack: plain-spoken and watchful, careful with a count, fond of a crisp apple and a storm heard from under sound tiles, and always carrying a faint smell of kiln smoke. Oath "Cracked and counted"; saying "Count it before the steward does".
- rm136 the herald's rehearsal: cries the callers' names at the great palace and practises every announcement aloud on the long climb up from the Commons: unhurried, warm and plain-spoken, careful with every name, fond of spiced cider, fresh straw underfoot and an old tale told properly. Oath "Hear and heed"; saying "A name said right is a kindness".
- rm137 the hiccup that isn't: worries about everything twice, lines things up in rows, swims in cold water without a word of complaint, and calls every cough a hiccup no matter who heard it. Oath "Oh, wet whiskers"; saying "Careful is quicker in the end".
- rm138 the winter forecaster: mucks out stables for a wage, keeps quiet, takes offence quickly and jokes drier than the hay, eats stale bread without a word, and gives the great Houses till winter to be at each other's throats. Oath "Muck and manger"; saying "Give it till winter".
- rm139 the thunder-counter: is a calm, serious young wolf of Northgate who counts between the flash and the bang in every storm, likes a crisp apple and a fair bout on the green, sits quiet in the empty chapel, and makes waiting bearable by counting it. Oath "Flash and bang"; saying "If you count it, it's farther off".
- rm140 the clerk of due dates: copies and files for the city and keeps every due date in a head built for it: dry, exact, quick to bristle at a slight, happiest digging a garden in a warm rain with a mug of spiced cider waiting indoors. Oath "In triplicate"; saying "Everything falls due".
- rm141 the stub-saver with the petition: is a patient, truthful pup of Northgate who says exactly what is so, keeps a box of every candle stub found on the floor, and goes back to the City Office window every single week to ask after a petition for a lamp on the dark corner. Oath "Wicks and whiskers"; saying "True is true".
- rm142 the square's echo: can do the cry of every stall in Market Square, and does, loudly: proud, quick to bristle, never short of words, mad for a Marketday crush, a mutton stew and the birds on the gutters, and nobody's beggar. Oath "Bawl it out"; saying "If it's worth a word, it's worth the whole square".
- rm143 the buckle-checker: carries letters through the north end of the city with a satchel whose buckle gets checked at every corner: quiet, tired and anxious, wary of crowds, strangers and wet floors, fond of plum tart, a hand of cards and old maps with the roads drawn wrong. Oath "Wax and buckle"; saying "A letter's only safe in the paw it's meant for".
- rm144 the puddle-mapper: chatters, fibs a little and owns up with a grin, whittles anything that holds still, and draws careful maps of where every puddle in the street runs off to. Oath "Chips and crackling"; saying "If it isn't on a map, it's mine".
- rm145 the label-turner: keeps a preserves stall on Market Square: quiet, patient and tired, honest to the lid, sworn to honey over sugar, ink on both forepaws from the labels, and forever turning every jar so its label faces the front. Oath "Skim and stir"; saying "Patience sets the jelly".
- rm146 the straightener: is a quiet, dry young wolf of a poor Northgate stair who straightens every crooked thing within reach without a word: tidy, unimpressed, fond of a cold swim, a loud storm and roast pork with the crackling on, and short with noise after dark. Oath "Corners and crackling"; saying "Crooked's only lazy straight".
- rm147 the hammer that answers the bell: works iron at the back of the shop and lives over it: slow-spoken, unhurried and quick to take offence at bad manners, honest at the scales, fond of the stars and the year's first snow, and given to striking the anvil once each time the chapel bell rings. Oath "Horn of the anvil"; saying "A rude word cools slower than iron".
