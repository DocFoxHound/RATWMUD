## Voices (one line each: what makes this wolf unlike any other in the town)
- sm123 the baker who asks whose colours: bakes before light and talks the whole time the dough proves: loud, quick to take offence, honest at the scales, and sure that anyone wearing a House's colours will sell you to another House by noon. Oath "Crust and cinders"; saying "Bread never asks whose colours you wear".
- sm124 the harvest-coal butcher: cuts meat for the market and will not be hurried: proud, stubborn and plain-spoken, keen of knife and short of patience, with a coal off a harvest fire carried in one pocket for luck. Oath "Hock and harvest"; saying "Cut once and mean it".
- lf139 the swearer who says sorry: sweeps and carries in a family shop on the Commons, whittles little sheep of an evening and begs the Bright One's pardon after every oath: gentle, devout and unhurried, never one to send a hungry wolf off empty. Oath "By the shepherd's crook"; saying "Bread shared is never stale".
- lf140 the crust-saver: cooks in a great kitchen up the hill, lives at the top of a long stair in the Sump and keeps a pocket of crusts for the roof birds: chatty, gentle and curious, glad of any face and any market crowd. Oath "Crumbs and chimneys"; saying "There's always a crumb for somebody".
- lf141 the plank-knocker: builds hulls on the slip and knocks on every plank before it goes in: short, plain and cold, proud of the yard, slow to trust another House's word, and never once heard to haggle. Oath "Keel and knuckle"; saying "Knock first, trust after".
- lf142 the door-minder who dreams uphill: minds a shop door in the Sump and knows every big house on the Heights by the knocker on its door: warm, plain and forever restless, putting every spare thought toward a room up the hill before the end. Oath "Brass and bolt"; saying "Every door opens for somebody".
- lf143 the chalk-tally crane hand: works a winding drum on the wharf and keeps every card debt in chalk on the nearest wall: warm, quick to snap and quicker to feed you, honest to the copper, and set on a dry room up the hill. Oath "By the jib and the drum"; saying "Chalk it up and it's owed".
- lf144 the load-blesser: lifts cargo on the wharf and says a blessing over every load before it leaves the ground: proud, devout and plain, short with sloppy work, keeper of a spotless room and a strong Restday singing voice. Oath "Bright One steady the rope"; saying "Bless it before it leaves the ground".
- lf145 the notched stick: hauls stone and mortar in a masons' yard and keeps a stick by the bed with a notch for every town still to be seen: devout, plain, restless, slow to speak, praying for patience and never quite getting it. Oath "Bright One hold the wall"; saying "A heavy stone's an honest one".
- sm125 the porter who hums the tide: carries loads across the market square from dawn and walks the city after dark for the pleasure of it: warm, plain, never still, humming some sea song under the breath that nobody taught on purpose. Oath "Strap and shoulder"; saying "Every load gets set down somewhere".
- lf146 the stamp-reader: mends pots and kettles at a bench in the Sump and turns each one over for the maker's stamp before looking at the hole: quiet, dry and quick to bristle, honest at the till, putting coppers by for a stall up on the Commons. Oath "Pewter and pins"; saying "A patch is honester than a promise".
- sm126 the door-minder at prayer: sits by the shop door through the morning with a prayer for everyone who passes and a shut door for anyone wearing a House's livery: warm, plain, devout, and homesick for the southern orchards. Oath "Bright One keep the bough"; saying "A shut door keeps more than draughts out".
- lf147 the lid-dater: sets fruit by the kettle and writes the day and the weather on every lid: warm, plain and earnest, one price and no haggling, saving for a second counter, fond of old tales and of maps that get the roads wrong. Oath "Ticket and twine"; saying "A written price saves a morning's talk".
- lf148 the weekly asker: minds a sundries counter in the Commons and goes back to the same Office window every week till it answers: warm and stubborn, quick to bristle and quicker to offer cheese, happiest in cold water or a warm rain. Oath "By the third window"; saying "Ask again; asking's free".
- lf149 the button-mender: sews in a tailor's back room in the Commons and says little: warm, quiet and close about personal matters, quick to sew a loose button back on for nothing, slow to tell the end of an old tale. Oath "Thimble and thread"; saying "A button's cheap till it's gone".
- lf150 the mark-reader: sits at a jeweller's bench reading the maker's mark on everything before saying a word: quiet, short-tempered, tired, plain as a band, trusting no House's word, warmed by spiced cider and long walks alone. Oath "Mark and metal"; saying "Read the back, then believe the front".
- lf151 the wheel-spinner: keeps a trade by the fish quay and keeps the door shut to strangers: cold, plain and restless, one price said once and never haggled, spinning every wheel within reach to hear whether it runs true. Oath "Rim and spoke"; saying "Round, or it's firewood".
- sm127 the chamber-keeper with a lucky lump: keeps the family's chambers in one of the great houses and keeps a lump of coal knotted in a handkerchief for luck: calm, patient, generous to a fault, and quietly sure the Houses will quarrel before the winter is out. Oath "Lump and linen"; saying "A polished floor tells no tales".
- sm128 the crier with chalk under the lid: cries laces, buttons and needles from a tray in the rag market and chalks every debt, owed or owing, on the underside of its lid: blunt, dry, honest to the penny, and fonder of a dance than anyone would guess. Oath "Buttons and blight"; saying "Every penny comes home to the lid".
- lf152 the burr-picker at the forge: shoes cart horses at a forge by the east gate and talks the whole while, loud and restless, plain as a nail, picking burrs out of a thick ruff mid-sentence and stealing off to sit in an empty chapel. Oath "Hammer and horseshoe"; saying "Every burr comes out in the end".
- lf153 the coin-weigher at the door: keeps watch at a shop door in the Commons and weighs every coin in the paw before it goes in: quiet, dry as chalk, plain-dealing, and still full of plans for a corner shop with the family name over it. Oath "Heft and halfpenny"; saying "Heavy coin, honest paw".
- sm129 the docker who touches the coal: unloads ships at the piers and touches a lump of coal in the coat pocket before every lift: calm, restless, few words, wary of the Watch, and saving for a bit of ground to dig out past the wall. Oath "Hold and heave"; saying "What goes in a hold comes out a hold".
- lf154 the carter who talks to milestones: drives a cart out of the city and back and nods good day to every milestone on the road as if it were a neighbour: quiet, calm and content, slow with words, keeping a satchel of old maps to dream over and a jar of pickled herring under the seat. Oath "Rut and reckoning"; saying "A stone that's stood that long has earned a nod".
- lf155 the churner who grades grandmothers: churns butter on the city's edge and grades every grandmother in earshot like a pound at market: quiet, guarded and tired, a master at the churn, fond of warm bread, a keen knife and a wrestling match, and close about everything else. Oath "Cream and crock"; saying "Butter comes when it's ready, not when you are".
- lf156 the picker with three stories: picks apples on the hills outside the city and lives poor in it: quiet, guarded and honest, low more often than not, fond of a hard old cheese and a warm rain, and forever telling the same three stories, the oldest about a ring of standing stones above home, said to remember every paw laid on it. Oath "Stone and windfall"; saying "The old stones forget nothing".
- lf157 the lamplighter who never haggles: lights the lamps of the Sump's back courts at dusk and climbs four flights to a top-floor room after: quiet, warm and loyal, guarded with strangers, fond of barley soup and a frosty morning, and the one wolf from a market town who pays the first price asked and will not haggle, ever. Oath "Spark and spill"; saying "The first price is the honest one".
- sm130 the censer-smoke chorister: sings in the cathedral choir and climbs home to a room in the Sump every night: talkative and quick to flare, plain-dealing, proud of a tidy room and a clean top note, and never free of the smell of censer smoke. Oath "Smoke and psalter"; saying "Hold the note and the note holds you".
- lf158 the brewer who notches the floods: brews and keeps a tap room in the Commons and lives over it: warm, devout and plain, quick to feed a stranger and slow to speak ill, with a floor swept twice a day and a cellar post notched with every flood the city has sent down the stairs. Oath "Barley and blessing"; saying "Water keeps its own ledger".
- lf159 the slow physician of the hill: climbs the hill each day with a physician's bag: quiet, unhurried and a little dreamy, mixing every remedy by hand and trusting none that came out of a House's still-room. Oath "Clean paws and patience"; saying "Rest does what physic can't".
- lf160 the cask-chalker: works the fruit press and sells what it makes over the counter: warm, plain and careful to a fault, feeding anyone who stands still, chalking the day on every cask and keeping a larder full shelf by shelf. Oath "Chalk and cask"; saying "Date it, or you're only guessing".
- sm131 the gate's map-mender: keeps a gate of the city and wants every passer's name and business: loud, wry and talkative, plain in dealing, with ink on both forepaws from setting old maps right. Oath "Bar and bolt"; saying "Name first, business after".
- lf161 the innkeeper who feeds the roof: keeps an inn and keeps it strictly: blunt, quick to take offence, fair to the penny at the till, and at closing throws the day's crusts up to the birds on the roof. Oath "By the pint and the pound"; saying "A full pint wants no excuses".
- lf162 the map-mender of the hall: keeps a great hall and its ledgers with a sharp eye and an even voice: patient, formal, never one to haggle, and fond of old maps, with the true roads pencilled in of an evening. Oath "By the true road"; saying "A price is named once in this house".
- sm132 the mud at the Court door: rides out on the House's business and comes home muddy to the hocks: easy-natured, kind and plain-spoken, assured in a quiet way, happier in a sheepfold than a hall, and forever scraping boots at the door. Oath "Hoof and hearth"; saying "Leave the mud at the door".
- lf163 the clerk with the margin sum: clerks at a desk by day and saves for a market stall coin by coin, keeping the running sum in the margin of whatever page is nearest: quiet, warm, dry as old paper, and fond of a tale told slowly. Oath "Margin and sum"; saying "Carry the one and keep going".
