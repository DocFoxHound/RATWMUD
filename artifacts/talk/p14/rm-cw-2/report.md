## Voices (one line each: what makes this wolf unlike any other in the town)
- cw189 the hive hand who trusts no wall: walks out through the city gate every morning to the hives on the heath and back in at dusk: wry, warm and a little low, suspicious of anything made, baked or said inside the walls, fond of an old tale and a buttered oatcake, and saving for boots nobody in the city cobbled. Oath "Swarm and settle"; saying "Walls keep in more than they keep out".
- cw190 the dreamer behind the syrup jars: helps behind the counter of a remedy shop, stirring cough syrups and handing out jars, humming old sea songs under the breath all day: quiet, frank and contented, a dreamer of far shores who can't abide the salt wind, fond of soused herring, a well-kept paring knife and the birds along the roofline. Oath "Haul away"; saying "A song goes where a body can't".
- cw191 the pip-saver of the press house: turns the big screw at a fruit press down in the Sump and comes home sticky to the elbows, chattering: gentle, merry and plain, a leg that aches when the weather turns, a twist of apple pips saved in a pocket for the garden to be planted one day up on the Heights. Oath "Pips and pressings"; saying "Up's the only road out of the Sump".
- cw192 the swimmer who swears it's warm: hauls sacks and minds the counter at a Sump stores, swims off the river steps whatever the weather and climbs out swearing the water's warm: short-tempered, dry and stubborn, plain-spoken, saving copper by copper for a stall up on the Commons, fond of mutton stew and old maps that get the roads wrong. Oath "Sacks and steps"; saying "Cold's only a word".
- cw193 the hummer on the back stair: is in service in a great household on the hill: chatty below stairs and correct above, ambitious for a steward's keys one day, fond of barley broth, a tidy room and a cold swim, and always humming an old sea song under the breath. Oath "Brass and beeswax"; saying "A house is run from the back stair".
- cw194 the rain-watcher at the bench: cuts and fits wood at a Commons bench and lives over the shop: devout, blunt and plain-spoken, a grace said over every board, a hard cheese at noon, and one eye always on the rain at the window, waiting for it to stop. Oath "Grain and grace"; saying "Rain finds the joint you skimped".
- cw195 the two-pen clerk: copies, files and stamps at a desk among the city's clerks: blunt, anxious and exact, frank to the point of rudeness, fond of a pickled herring, a game of dice and a well-stocked larder, and never without a spare pen behind each ear. Oath "Blot and bother"; saying "Written down is half done".
- cw196 the coal-giver at the pawn counter: helps behind a pawn counter in the low streets: chatty and merry, honest to a fault about what a thing is worth, fond of a sour apple, the birds on the roofs and an autumn blow, with a bad knee that reads the weather and a pocketful of coal, a lump of which goes to anyone who pawns a treasured thing. Oath "Pledge and penny"; saying "Pawned isn't parted".
- cw197 the rain-watcher on the step: sits at a window or on a front step in the city's middle streets, watching the rain and betting oatcakes on when it will stop: proud, shrewd and short of patience, fond of oatcakes, an old tale and fresh straw, quick to hint and slow to tell, and keeping one secret from a great hall on the hill. Oath "Rot the rain"; saying "It stops when it stops".
- cw198 the doorkeeper with the coal: minds a shop door and keeps its step swept: careful, anxious and plain, formal in the manner of the hill, noting every face that comes through, with a lump of coal in a paper twist in one pocket for luck. Oath "Hinge and latch"; saying "Wipe your paws and mind your luck".
- cw199 the link-tier on the square: makes and sells sausages and puddings off a stall on a city square: quiet and nervy, plain at the board, never haggling, fond of a hard cheese, an empty chapel and a song in company, and forever running up stairs as if already late. Oath "Salt and casing"; saying "Slow at the mincer, quick on the stair".
- cw200 the keeper of the letter: keeps the chambers of a great house on the Estate: quick and merry, cool underneath, ready to talk and slow to tell, swearing by forge and sea in one breath, with a letter in the apron for a master who is never home. Oath "Bellows and breakers"; saying "Polish what you see, pocket what you hear".
- cw201 the windowsill gardener: mixes lime and plaster and lives down in the low streets: quiet, unhurried and plain, short of coin and long on patience, coaxing something green out of every windowsill. Oath "Dust and daylight"; saying "It sets when it sets".
- cw202 the berth-book clerk: clerks the berths and dues of a busy wharf: chatty, wry and plain, quick with a joke and slow with a blot, able to name every ship that comes into the Sound, with a weakness for warm bread and the stars. Oath "Quill and keel"; saying "If it's not in the book, it never docked".
- cw203 the stall-jar keeper: sits at a window in the Works and watches the street go by, puts one copper in a jar every night toward a market stall, and wins at cards more than is polite: proud, tired, sharp-tongued and plain, with no use at all for pity. Oath "Frost and kettle"; saying "A copper a night and the jar fills".
- cw204 the prayer-stone picker: walks out of the city to the orchards and back each day, picking a smooth stone off the riverbank to hold through every prayer and setting it back in the water once the prayer is answered: quiet, devout, reserved and tired, glad of plum tart, a borrowed spade and the feast bells. Oath "Bright One walk beside me"; saying "Hold it till it's answered, then give it back".
- cw205 the jumpy fishmonger: keeps a fish slab in the Commons and lives up over it: chatty, jumpy at every bell and the first to laugh about it, plain at the scales, sure the Houses will fall out before winter, and weak for plum tart and new boots. Oath "Gills and gravy"; saying "A fish can't lie about its eyes".
- cw206 the roof-bird watcher: helps behind a butcher's counter in the Sump and lives four flights up, where the roof birds can be watched from the window: wary, cold, quick to see a bad sign, slow to trust a new face, and soothed only by warm bread and the first snow. Oath "Wing and weather"; saying "When the roof goes quiet, look to your door".
- cw207 the riddling butcher: keeps a butcher's counter down in the Sump: unhurried, warm and plain, quick to slip a scrag end into a thin basket, with a riddle chalked by the door each morning and every ship in the Sound known by what its galley cook comes up the stair to buy. Oath "Salt and slow fire"; saying "A full larder keeps a calm house".
- cw208 the almoner in oilcloth: hands out bread at a door and never steps outside without an oilcloth hood, rain or shine: blunt, short-fused and honest to the crumb, stubborn as a stuck oar, happiest with a line in the water on a long summer evening, and set on a berth south one day. Oath "Bowl and bread"; saying "Give it plain and give it now".
- cw209 the floor-proud server: serves in a great hall and keeps its flagstones spotless, with a cough in the damp that is never, ever a cough: proud, sharp, quick to take offence, ambitious past the hall, fond of plum tart and an old map of the south, and saving for a passage somewhere dry. Oath "Scrub me raw"; saying "Done is done, so don't walk on it".
- cw210 the favour-keeper: forgets no favour, given or owed, and can name the day each was done: gracious and assured in company, sly beneath the courtesy, restless behind tall windows, fond of crisp apples, old maps with wrong roads, and a good autumn wind. Oath "By the seal and the sum"; saying "A favour kept is a door left ajar".
- cw211 the knife-row keeper: helps behind a butcher's counter in the Sump and lays every knife out by length before the first cut: careful, warm and plain, loyal to whoever gave the work, and fond of honey cakes, a slow game of draughts and the long light of a summer evening. Oath "Edge and order"; saying "Every knife in its own place".
- cw212 the hummer who times the griddle: hums old sea songs under the breath and times every task by the tunes, two verses to an oatcake side: exact, guarded and civil, saying little of anyone, fond of oatcakes, the first stars of an evening, and the bells on a feast morning. Oath "Flour and fathoms"; saying "Two verses a side and never peek".
- cw213 the stair-racer in a clean coat: takes every stair at a run and lets nobody by, and keeps a coat so clean it shines: proud, prickly and quick to bristle, homesick for the hush of the charcoal mounds in the hills, fond of warm bread, a feast-night dance and a misty dawn. Oath "By the cold mound"; saying "Nobody passes me on a stair".
- cw214 the square hand: writes a careful, square hand, finds any paper given time, and blames every cough on the dust of old papers: gentle, patient and quietly proud, fond of old tales told slow, plain oatcakes, new straw and the first snow of the year. Oath "By the square hand"; saying "It's written down somewhere".
