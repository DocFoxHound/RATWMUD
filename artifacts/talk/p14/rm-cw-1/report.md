## Voices (one line each: what makes this wolf unlike any other in the town)
- cw163 the tray-crier: walks the rag stalls of the city with a tray of laces and buttons, crying wares to old lake tunes: quiet, short of temper, guarded, fond of a wrestling bout and a thunderstorm from under a roof, and carrying a letter nobody will take. Oath "Needles and nails"; saying "A shout sells, a whisper steals".
- cw164 the warden who owes nothing: keeps a hall of stalls in the city and keeps every debt in mind, owed or owing, to the copper: warm, anxious, plain-spoken, fond of a riddle and a jar of pickled herring, and forever asking after a petition lost somewhere in the City Office. Oath "Lake and ledger"; saying "A debt floats till it's paid".
- cw165 the bone-giver: cuts and hangs the meat in a Sump butcher's, gives the bones away for nothing and remembers every paw that took one: cold, guarded, plain, generous without ever saying so, fond of mutton stew, a throw of the dice and an empty chapel. Oath "Hook and hock"; saying "Bones go free, debts don't".
- cw166 the hummer under the slates: lives at the top of a Sump stair and works a city trade with a quiet pride, humming old rowing songs under the breath: patient, reserved, plain, slow to complain and slower to forgive, with a cup of spiced cider the one luxury kept. Oath "By the long oar"; saying "A song weighs nothing in the pocket".
- cw167 the lamplighter who counts the drowned: walks a round of street lamps at dusk with a pole and an oil can, counting every lamp the rain puts out: calm, plain, reserved, set on rising, missing the quiet of the woods above the lake, and never heard to complain. Oath "Oil and flint"; saying "Rain puts them out, I put them back".
- cw168 the hour-keeper at the needle: sews in a tailor's back room in the Works and counts every bell off the chapel against the hours owed: suspicious, cold to strangers, coat always brushed, and off to the Office each week about a paper nobody can find. Oath "Bell and bobbin"; saying "Count it yourself or it's not counted".
- cw169 the rain-bettor: helps in the family shop in the Commons and watches every shower from the doorway as if it must stop any moment, a copper on it: warm, chatty, quick to tease, fond of a throw of the dice, set on ending up somewhere grand. Oath "Bless the gutters"; saying "It's only passing through".
- cw170 the berth-keeper: keeps the harbour's berths and its ledger in a slow, quiet hand: calm, plain-spoken, tired at the edges, never without a little blade and a wet stone, and three strokes on it at every turn of the tide. Oath "Ebb and edge"; saying "Tie it so it'll untie".
- cw171 the dry-day marker: keeps a spotless room in the Commons and watches every shower from the window as if it might stop, ready to cut one more dry-day mark into the frame: gentle, plain and careful, cards on the table, a pot of mutton stew on the hob. Oath "Bright One dry the step"; saying "Count the dry days; the wet ones count themselves".
- cw172 the coal in the pocket: helps behind a butcher's counter in the city and keeps a few lumps of coal in a pocket for luck: calm, warm and dry, fond of dice, warm bread and a good thunderstorm, and happiest in an empty chapel. Oath "Coal in my pocket"; saying "Luck's heavier than it looks".
- cw173 the square accounts: hauls and slakes lime at a building yard and lives low in the city: careful, anxious and plain, forever warning folk off wet floors and hot kilns, and able to name every penny or favour owed, either way, back years. Oath "Lime in my eye"; saying "Short accounts and dry floors".
- cw174 the lime-burner in the wind: keeps the lime kilns burning for the House that owns the kilns and carries the smoke home in the fur: quiet, guarded and tired, the floor kept dry and strawed, a mug of spiced cider at dusk, eyes on the birds along the roofs. Oath "By the cold draw"; saying "Lime never forgets a wet paw".
- cw175 the swept step: sweeps and carries at a city works and keeps two rooms in the Sump spotless: chatty, restless and earnest, always with a plan and a horehound drop, keen on a bout of wrestling, and never heard complaining in the street. Oath "Grip and give"; saying "Complain at home, not in the street".
- cw176 the smith who never coughs: beats out anchor chain and minds a counter in the city's works: chatty and dry, plain at the scales, never far from a wedge of hard cheese, and coughing through every damp spell while swearing blind it's the smoke. Oath "Anvil and lake-ice"; saying "The weak link's always the quiet one".
- cw177 the humming stair-climber: helps behind a shop counter and lives at the top of a Sump stair: warm, plain and restless, humming the middle of some sea song whose end nobody knows, and forever back at the Office asking after a lost paper. Oath "Tar and tune"; saying "Hum it and it's half done".
- cw178 the hooded counter-keeper: keeps the counter of a family workshop in the Commons: guarded and plain-spoken, loud about manners and silent about everything else, never out of doors without an oilcloth hood, and the first on the green when the feast-day bells ring. Oath "Oilcloth and oak"; saying "A shut mouth catches no rain".
- cw179 the humming preserver: keeps a preserves counter under the market hall roof: chatty and dry, honest at the jar, fond of spiced cider and a clear sky, and humming a scrap of tune whenever the damp gets at the throat. Oath "By the wax seal"; saying "Nothing keeps that wasn't put up right".
- cw180 the bell-counting founder: runs a foundry in the Commons for a House that counts every farthing: stubborn, wry and plain, short with fools, forever counting the bells off the tide chapel, with an old map in one pocket and a letter nobody is home to take in the other. Oath "Slag and still water"; saying "Cool it slow or it cracks".
- cw181 the wick-trimmer: makes candles and lamp oil and lives over the shop: quiet, dry and watchful, slow to speak and quick to notice, with a pocket knife ready for any wick that smokes. Oath "Tallow and tinder"; saying "Trim it before it smokes".
- cw182 the knot-tally ropemaker: lays rope on the long walk down by the works: cold, careful and guarded, slow to trust a stranger, and keeps a cord in one pocket with a knot on it for every debt, owed or owing, till it's paid and picked out. Oath "Strand and splice"; saying "Paid is paid, and owed is owed".
- cw183 the high-shelf provisioner: keeps a provision store by the bridge and lives up the stair over it: quiet, warm and bone-tired, loyal to the ones who work alongside, with every sack on a high shelf and a map in the head of which streets go under first. Oath "Gutter and grate"; saying "Rain goes where it went last time".
- cw184 the forge-and-sea docker: hauls cargo off the ships at the piers and lives up a Sump stair: talkative, blunt and honest to a fault, swearing by forge and sea in one breath, tying fancy knots in any bit of line, and putting coppers by for a market stall. Oath "Forge and sea"; saying "What the sea brings, a back carries".
- cw185 the nib-sharpener: copies and files in a city office with a small wet stone at the elbow, never writing a word with a blunt quill: warm under a careful manner, guarded, exact, fond of smoked eel and a fishing line at dusk, and quietly dreaming of passage to somewhere dry. Oath "Blot and sand"; saying "Nothing's lost, only filed wrong".
- cw186 the drip-counter at the block: cuts and hangs meat at a butcher's in the Sump and counts the drips off the awning between customers, sure each shower is the last: quiet, guarded, tidy to the bone, and talking of the trade as if born to it. Oath "Block and cleaver"; saying "Sharp knife, shut mouth".
- cw187 the hull-namer: works the tar-pot and the counter at a wharf chandlery: loud, merry and quick to bristle, proud of naming every hull in the Sound by rig and wake at half a mile, and fond of draughts, thunder and an empty chapel. Oath "Tar and thunder"; saying "Paint lies, rigging doesn't".
- cw188 the bathhouse reckoner: keeps the fires and the tubs going at a city bathhouse: plain-spoken, anxious and driven, forever counting coppers under the breath, and never complaining where anyone can hear. Oath "Soap and steam"; saying "Hot water won't wait, and nor will a debt".
